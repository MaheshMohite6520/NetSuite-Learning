# Week 22 – Tax Architecture Deep Dive

## Overview

This week focuses on taking a **deep dive into NetSuite's Tax Architecture**, exploring how taxation is designed, calculated, validated, automated, reported, and governed across multiple jurisdictions using both **Legacy Tax** and **SuiteTax**.

Taxation is one of the most critical components of every ERP implementation.

It is far more than assigning a tax rate to a transaction.

It is a complete architecture connecting:

- Legacy Tax
- SuiteTax
- Nexus
- Tax Registrations
- Tax Codes
- Tax Types
- Tax Groups
- Tax Schedules
- Tax Engines
- Tax Agencies
- Tax Details
- Nexus Determination
- Tax Calculation
- Tax Exemptions
- Tax Reporting
- Tax Compliance
- Tax Migration
- General Ledger Integration
- Financial Reporting
- Multi-Jurisdiction Taxation
- SuiteScript Tax Automation
- Third-Party Tax Integrations
- Audit Trail Management
- Financial Governance

Every taxable transaction eventually impacts financial statements.

Whether the transaction originates from:

- Sales Orders
- Invoices
- Cash Sales
- Purchase Orders
- Vendor Bills
- Credit Memos
- Return Authorizations
- Item Fulfillments
- Intercompany Transactions
- Service Transactions
- Subscription Billing
- Project Billing
- International Sales
- Multi-Subsidiary Operations

the organization must ultimately answer one critical question:

> Which tax jurisdiction applies, and how should the tax be calculated accurately and compliantly?

Because taxation is never determined solely by a percentage.

It depends on jurisdictions, nexus rules, registrations, tax engines, transaction details, exemptions, and regulatory compliance.
